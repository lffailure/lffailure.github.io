Stitching expert continuations onto failed policy rollouts. We use failed rollouts to select where expert supervision is queried, and stitch the resulting expert continuation to the preceding policy context.

Abstract

Imitation learning has produced visuomotor policies that handle an increasingly broad range of manipulation tasks. They are typically trained on successful trajectories, which show how to complete the task but reveal less about how to act once execution leaves that distribution. This gap is costly in articulated object manipulation, where small grasping or control errors compound under sustained contact and kinematic constraints. We present a failure-triggered data aggregation framework that turns failed policy rollouts into stitched recovery trajectories. For each failed rollout, we re-execute the heuristic expert from a failure point selected by a vision-language model and stitch its continuation onto the policy prefix. Expert supervision is thus requested only at failures, once per episode, and entirely offline. Across 18 articulated objects under held-out configurations, this improves average task success from 22.3% to 34.2%. The gain depends on keeping the failure prefix: the expert continuation alone recovers little of it, and it persists when the trajectory count is held fixed.

Method

  1. Train a baseline policy. A 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) π0 is trained on successful demonstrations DB from a heuristic expert that reads privileged simulator state (object mesh and joint axis).
  2. Collect rollouts and split by outcome. π0 is rolled out without intervention. Successful rollouts are kept directly as DS; failed rollouts DF become the source of recovery supervision.
  3. Select a cut point with a VLM. 25 frames of each failed rollout are arranged into a 5×5 contact sheet, and Qwen2-VL picks the frame where the failure begins. The VLM produces no robot actions and is not used at test time.
  4. Re-execute the expert and stitch. The robot and object configuration at the cut point is restored, the same heuristic expert is re-executed, and its continuation is stitched onto the policy prefix. Failed continuations and trajectories with large end-effector discontinuities are discarded.
  5. Retrain. A new policy is trained from scratch on DB ∪ DS ∪ DFR, with the optimizer-step budget matched to the baseline (~72k steps).

Results

We evaluate on 18 PartNet-Mobility objects with revolute and prismatic joints in PyBullet. Demonstrations and evaluation use disjoint sets of randomized configurations (object pose and scale, robot initialization, initial articulation state), so all numbers measure generalization to held-out configurations. Every condition is trained with 3 seeds and evaluated on 50 rollouts per object; we report mean ± std over seeds.

Improvement across objects

The full method B+S+FR improves average success from 22.3% to 34.2% (+11.9 pp) and improves on all 18 objects. Since all added data comes from the data-collection configurations, the gain reflects generalization to the held-out set.

Limitations

Our evaluation is limited to simulation, and the data-generation pipeline is tied to it: recovery generation restores a recorded simulator state, and the heuristic expert reads geometry and joint parameters a physical system cannot access. These requirements apply only to data generation. The trained policy acts from point clouds and proprioception, so it can run on a real robot without the simulator. Evaluating that transfer on real articulated objects is our immediate next step, while extending the generation pipeline itself to hardware remains a harder problem.
